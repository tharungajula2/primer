Indian lending lens: RBI lender-specific prudential rules + directed-credit obligations + conduct rules + stressed-asset resolution and enforcement.

```text
Identify the regulated lender and governing regime
→
Determine what it may lend and its directed-credit obligations
→
Set product terms, price and borrower disclosures
→
Obtain credit information and consented financial data
→
Deliver directly, digitally or through co-lending arrangements
→
Detect missed payment and start the regulatory stress clock
→
Apply the product-specific NPA test and borrower-wise classification
→
Provision, recognise income and determine cure or restructuring treatment
→
Choose resolution, enforcement, settlement and collection route
→
Keep the operative IRACP regime distinct from the 1 April 2027 ECL transition
```

**Permanent rule: In Indian lending, the treatment of a loan is determined by the regulated lender, the product-specific rule and the borrower-level asset state, under the RBI regime in force on that date.**

## 1. RBI governs lending through prudential, conduct and directed-credit rules

Indian lending is governed through several regulatory levers at once. Prudential rules determine asset classification, income recognition, provisioning, large-exposure treatment and resolution. Conduct rules determine disclosure, pricing transparency, outsourcing, collections and grievance redress. Directed-credit rules require specified lenders to allocate part of their lending to priority sectors.

These levers are cumulative. A qualifying agriculture loan can count towards priority-sector targets and still become an NPA under the applicable asset-quality rule. A digitally originated loan remains subject to the same prudential treatment as an equivalent branch-originated loan. An outsourced recovery process remains the regulated entity's responsibility.

The consequence is structural. Indian lending cannot be read as one master rulebook. The lender category is identified first. The product and channel are identified next. The applicable RBI direction then supplies the operative rule.

### Mental model

Regulation answers three different questions: **may this entity lend, how must this loan be delivered, and how must this exposure be treated after stress**. Mixing those questions is the source of many false shortcuts.

## 2. Lender type fixes the legal perimeter of lending

The same borrower can face different regulatory treatment depending on the lender. The first classification is therefore the lender, not the loan.

| Lender | May lend from own balance sheet? | Core perimeter | Main lending regime in practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial bank | Yes | Full banking and lending powers | Banking Regulation Act + RBI commercial-bank directions |
| Small Finance Bank | Yes | Full banking with inclusion-focused licensing conditions | RBI bank directions + SFB-specific licensing and PSL rules |
| Payments Bank | No | Deposits, payments and permitted financial services; no lending from own balance sheet | Payments Bank licensing conditions and RBI directions |
| NBFC-BL | Yes for ordinary lending NBFCs; activity-specific NBFCs may not | Non-bank credit with Base Layer prudential requirements | RBI Scale Based Regulation framework |
| NBFC-ML | Yes for lending NBFCs | Larger, deposit-taking or specified-category NBFCs | RBI Scale Based Regulation framework with tighter prudential rules |
| NBFC-UL or TL | Yes where licensed activity permits | Systemically significant NBFCs | Enhanced RBI prudential and governance requirements |
| Housing Finance Company | Yes | Housing finance as an RBI-regulated NBFC category | HFC directions + applicable SBR layer |
| Urban co-operative bank | Yes | Banking and lending within co-operative and RBI perimeter | Banking Regulation Act as applicable + RBI UCB directions |
| Rural co-operative institution | Yes within statutory mandate | Rural and co-operative credit | Co-operative law, NABARD role and RBI banking regulation where applicable |

A scheduled status does not by itself describe lending powers. A payments bank may be scheduled yet cannot lend. An NBFC may lend without accepting demand deposits. An HFC is not a separate prudential universe outside RBI; it is an NBFC category with housing-finance-specific rules.

## 3. NBFC scale changes regulatory intensity, not the nature of the lender

RBI's Scale Based Regulation framework layers NBFCs by size, activity and systemic significance.

```text
NBFC universe
→
Base Layer
ordinary non-deposit NBFCs below the Middle Layer threshold
plus specified activity-based categories
→
Middle Layer
all deposit-taking NBFCs
larger non-deposit NBFCs
HFCs, CICs, IFCs and other specified categories
→
Upper Layer
NBFCs identified by RBI through size and risk-based scoring
→
Top Layer
exceptional supervisory category for materially higher systemic risk
```

The Base Layer is not synonymous with small lenders. Some specialised NBFC categories sit there because of activity. The Middle Layer captures all deposit-taking NBFCs and non-deposit NBFCs at or above the prescribed asset threshold, together with specified categories such as HFCs. The Upper Layer is selected by RBI rather than entered automatically at one simple balance-sheet size.

The layer changes capital, governance, exposure, disclosure and prudential requirements. It does not convert an NBFC into a bank. Demand-deposit, payment-system and deposit-insurance distinctions remain.

### Permanent trap

Applying one NBFC rule to the whole sector. Layer, activity and deposit-taking status must be identified before a prudential requirement is applied.

## 4. Priority sector is a portfolio obligation, not a product label

Priority Sector Lending is a regulatory allocation rule. It measures qualifying credit against a prescribed base, generally Adjusted Net Bank Credit or the credit-equivalent amount of off-balance-sheet exposure, whichever is higher under the applicable direction.

A loan does not become priority-sector credit because it is small, rural or socially useful. It must satisfy the RBI definition of a qualifying category. Agriculture, MSME, export credit, education, housing, social infrastructure, renewable energy and specified weaker-section lending are separate eligibility routes. Several categories carry internal sub-targets.

The obligation is tested at lender level. A bank can therefore make a sound qualifying loan and still miss its overall target because another sub-target is short. Conversely, excess achievement in one permitted bucket may support Priority Sector Lending Certificate activity without transferring the underlying loan.

The operative logic is:

```text
Identify lender type
→
Determine total PSL target
→
Classify each eligible exposure by RBI category
→
Test sub-targets
→
Measure shortfall or surplus
→
Use permitted PSLC or other regulatory mechanisms where relevant
```

PSL changes portfolio construction. It does not change the borrower's contractual obligation or the prudential classification of the loan after stress.

## 5. Priority-sector targets differ by lender type

The target matrix must be read by lender type. Percentages are measured against the RBI-prescribed base for that lender.

| Lender type | Overall PSL | Agriculture | Small and marginal farmers | Micro enterprises | Weaker sections |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Domestic commercial banks | 40% | 18% | 10% | 7.5% | 12% |
| Foreign banks with 20 or more branches | 40% | 18% | 10% | 7.5% | 12% |
| Small Finance Banks | 75% | 18% | 10% | 7.5% | 12% |
| Regional Rural Banks | 75% | 18% | 10% | 7.5% | 15% |
| Urban co-operative banks | 60% | As prescribed for the category | As prescribed | 7.5% | 12% |
| Foreign banks with fewer than 20 branches | 40% | Separate composition rules apply | Separate composition rules apply | Separate composition rules apply | Separate composition rules apply |

Agriculture includes a separate non-corporate farmer component and other internal composition requirements under the operative PSL direction. The headline percentages above do not replace the category definitions.

A target table is a compliance map, not a lending strategy. It does not answer whether a proposed loan qualifies. That is settled by the borrower, purpose, amount and category tests.

### Permanent trap

Treating the 40% or 75% overall target as the only test. A lender can meet the headline percentage and still fail a mandatory agriculture, small-farmer, micro-enterprise or weaker-section sub-target.

## 6. PSL eligibility turns on category definitions and prescribed limits

Qualification is a rule-based classification exercise.

| Category | Core eligibility logic | Common boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture | Credit to individual farmers, farmer collectives and permitted agriculture or allied activities | Purpose and borrower type matter; not every rural loan qualifies |
| Small and marginal farmers | Qualifying agriculture exposure to the RBI-defined small or marginal farmer population | It is a sub-set of agriculture, not an additional loan category |
| MSME | Credit to an enterprise falling within the notified micro, small or medium classification | Enterprise classification is determined under the current Government of India MSME criteria |
| Micro enterprises | Qualifying lending to micro enterprises | Separate PSL sub-target applies |
| Weaker sections | Lending to the borrower classes listed by RBI, including specified low-income and vulnerable groups | It overlaps other PSL categories but is measured as a separate sub-target |
| Education | Qualifying education loans within the prescribed amount and purpose limits | Large education exposures outside the prescribed ceiling do not qualify |
| Housing | Qualifying housing loans subject to location, loan-size and dwelling-cost ceilings | A home loan is not automatically PSL |
| Renewable energy | Permitted renewable-energy financing within the prescribed borrower limits | Large project finance can fall outside PSL even when the activity is renewable |
| Social infrastructure | Permitted facilities such as schools, health facilities and sanitation infrastructure within prescribed limits | Purpose and amount thresholds govern eligibility |

For MSMEs, the enterprise classification in force from 1 April 2025 uses both investment and turnover. Micro enterprises are capped at ₹2.5 crore investment and ₹10 crore turnover; small enterprises at ₹25 crore and ₹100 crore; medium enterprises at ₹125 crore and ₹500 crore.

The PSL decision is made from current facts. A change in enterprise classification or use of funds can change future eligibility without changing the loan contract itself.

## 7. PSLCs transfer target achievement, not loans or credit risk

### Never confuse PSLCs and loan transfers

**Priority Sector Lending Certificate**

A PSLC transfers regulatory achievement for a specified PSL category for the relevant period. The originating lender retains the loan, borrower relationship, credit risk and cash flows. The certificate has no underlying transfer of principal.

**Loan transfer**

A loan transfer moves an economic interest in a credit exposure under the applicable transfer rules. It changes who holds the asset or a share of it. Credit risk and cash-flow rights move to the extent transferred.

The distinction matters because PSLC trading cannot repair poor asset quality. A bank that buys PSLCs can improve PSL compliance without acquiring the seller's borrowers. A bank that sells PSLCs does not reduce its advances or remove the associated NPAs from its books.

PSLC categories are structured around the regulatory shortfall being addressed, including agriculture, small and marginal farmers, micro enterprises and general PSL. The price is negotiated between participating institutions. The certificate is therefore a compliance instrument, not a credit instrument.

## 8. Sanction must expose price, charges and repayment obligations

Origination controls are layered. The sanction communication states the contractual terms. The Key Facts Statement standardises the cost and repayment information for covered retail and MSME term loans.

| Control | What must be clear before the borrower is bound | Regulatory consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Sanction terms | Amount, tenor, interest basis, repayment structure, security and material conditions | The lender cannot rely on undisclosed material terms |
| Interest-rate basis | Fixed or floating rate, benchmark where applicable, spread and reset mechanism | Repricing must follow the disclosed method |
| Fees and charges | Processing, documentation, insurance or other borrower-borne charges | Undisclosed covered charges cannot be imposed later |
| Penal charges | Trigger, amount or method and application | Penal charges cannot be disguised as additional penal interest |
| Prepayment or foreclosure | Conditions and any permitted charge | Treatment must follow the applicable RBI borrower-protection rule |
| KFS | APR, repayment schedule, key charges and grievance details for covered loans | KFS becomes the compact economic disclosure |
| Recovery and grievance | Recovery contact and escalation route | Outsourcing does not remove lender accountability |

The disclosure rule is substantive. A fee recovered by a third party on behalf of the lender remains part of the borrower's credit economics where the KFS framework requires inclusion. A term added after sanction requires a lawful contractual basis and, where applicable, borrower consent.

## 9. Floating-rate pricing is benchmark plus lender spread

For covered floating-rate loans of scheduled commercial banks, the contractual rate is built from an external benchmark and a lender spread.

```text
Borrower Rate = External Benchmark + Spread

Borrower Rate       contractual annual interest rate
External Benchmark  RBI-permitted published benchmark for the product
Spread              lender component reflecting credit and permitted operating factors

Worked: 5.50% benchmark + 2.40% spread = 7.90% annual borrower rate
```

Permitted external benchmarks include the policy repo rate and specified market benchmarks published by the Financial Benchmarks India Private Limited framework. Covered loans include new floating-rate personal and retail loans and floating-rate loans to MSMEs under the RBI external-benchmark regime.

The benchmark must reset at least once in three months. A benchmark move changes the rate through the contractual formula. The spread is not a second floating benchmark that can be altered without the conditions stated in RBI rules and the loan contract.

The rule does not mean every Indian loan is repo-linked. NBFC pricing and products outside the scheduled-commercial-bank benchmark requirement follow their own regulatory and contractual framework.

### Key insight

External benchmarking makes repricing observable. It does not make credit spreads identical across borrowers or lenders.

## 10. The KFS converts loan economics into a comparable annual cost

The Key Facts Statement compresses the economics of a covered loan into a standard borrower-facing disclosure. The Annual Percentage Rate incorporates interest and the charges that the RBI methodology requires to be included.

```text
Net Amount Received = Sanctioned Amount - Upfront Charges Deducted

Total Borrower Cost = Scheduled Interest + Included Charges

APR = annualised rate that equates the net amount received with the scheduled borrower cash outflows

Sanctioned Amount  contractual principal
Upfront Charges    included charges deducted before or at disbursal
Borrower Cashflows principal, interest and included charges over the tenor
APR                annualised all-in cost under the RBI KFS methodology

Worked: ₹5,00,000 sanctioned - ₹5,000 included upfront charges = ₹4,95,000 net amount received. APR is calculated from ₹4,95,000 against the full scheduled repayment cashflows.
```

The KFS also carries the amortisation schedule and key charges. A covered fee not mentioned in the KFS cannot simply appear later without the treatment permitted by the directions. Third-party charges included in APR are passed through on an actual basis and supported by receipts or documents where required.

APR is not the same as the nominal interest rate. Two loans at the same stated rate can have different APRs because their included charges or repayment timing differ.

## 11. Fair-practice duties survive channel and outsourcing

### Permanent rule

A regulated lender remains responsible for the lending conduct performed in its name, including conduct performed through an outsourced service provider.

A branch, web journey, mobile application, direct-selling agent, recovery agent and Lending Service Provider are channels or agents. None creates a separate prudential borrower relationship outside the regulated entity.

The practical consequences are direct. Sanction terms remain the lender's terms. Grievance escalation remains available against the regulated entity. Recovery behaviour by an appointed agent can trigger supervisory consequences for the lender. A technology provider cannot collect undisclosed charges merely because the fee appears in an app rather than a sanction letter.

The same principle controls digital lending. The LSP can perform permitted functions such as customer acquisition, application support, servicing or recovery. It cannot become an unregulated substitute lender merely because it controls the borrower interface.

### Permanent trap

Treating outsourcing as transfer of accountability. Contractual delegation changes who performs a task. It does not remove the regulated entity's responsibility for the regulatory outcome.

## 12. Credit information and consented cash-flow data are different evidence channels

Credit bureaus and consented-data rails answer different questions.

| Evidence channel | What it contains | What it is used for | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit Information Company | Reported loans, limits, repayment history, enquiries, defaults and related credit data | Existing credit behaviour and indebtedness | Current business cash generation |
| Credit score | Modelled summary of bureau information | Fast risk ranking and policy cut-offs | Ability to repay a specific proposed loan by itself |
| Account Aggregator data | Customer-consented financial information from participating providers | Bank-flow, investment or other cash-flow evidence | Credit approval; the AA does not underwrite |
| GST-derived information | Turnover, tax filing and transaction evidence available through permitted channels | Business activity and cash-flow corroboration | Profitability or genuine repayment capacity without analysis |
| Internal account data | Existing lender transaction and repayment behaviour | Behavioural assessment and servicing | External indebtedness not visible to the lender |

Credit Information Companies operate under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 and RBI directions. Banks and NBFCs report credit information and use bureau data as a standard credit input. The score is not a regulatory guarantee of creditworthiness.

Cash-flow data is complementary. It can reduce reliance on manually supplied statements, but the lending decision remains with the regulated lender.

## 13. Account Aggregators move borrower-permissioned evidence, not credit decisions

An Account Aggregator is an RBI-regulated consent manager and data intermediary. It does not lend and does not decide whether credit should be granted.

```text
Customer
→ gives a specific consent artefact
Account Aggregator
→ requests permitted data
Financial Information Provider
→ sends encrypted data through the AA framework
Financial Information User lender
→ receives the permitted information
Credit process
→ analyses the information and makes its own decision
```

The consent identifies the information requested, purpose, frequency and duration. The framework is designed so that the AA moves information under customer authority rather than becoming an uncontrolled data warehouse.

For MSME lending, bank-account and GST-linked information can support cash-flow assessment. It can show turnover patterns, seasonality, collections and obligations more quickly than repeated document requests. The evidentiary value depends on completeness and interpretation. A high bank inflow is not automatically operating cash flow. GST turnover is not automatically free cash available for debt service.

### Key insight

The AA framework changes the cost and speed of evidence collection. It does not change who bears underwriting responsibility.

## 14. Digital lending leaves lender accountability with the regulated entity

The Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 organise digital lending around three objects.

A **Regulated Entity**, or RE, is the bank or NBFC that legally lends. A **Lending Service Provider**, or LSP, performs one or more functions for the RE in the digital lending process. A **Digital Lending App**, or DLA, is the digital interface through which the activity is carried out.

The loan remains on the RE's books unless transferred under another permitted framework. The RE performs due diligence on the LSP, monitors its conduct and remains responsible for compliance. The LSP can distribute, support underwriting inputs, service or recover within the contractual and regulatory perimeter. It cannot use its interface to bypass KFS, borrower-consent, data, fund-flow or grievance rules.

Where an LSP presents offers from multiple lenders, the presentation must follow the RBI comparison and disclosure requirements rather than steer the borrower through undisclosed commercial bias.

Digital lending is therefore a delivery model. It does not create a separate asset-classification regime. The same loan still moves through overdue, SMA and NPA rules under the lender's applicable prudential direction.

## 15. The RE-LSP control boundary is explicit

The operational split is controlled rather than informal.

| RE must | LSP may | Prohibited or constrained |
|---|---|---|
| Perform due diligence before engaging an LSP | Source and assist applicants under mandate | Present itself as the balance-sheet lender when it is not |
| Remain responsible for regulatory compliance | Collect application information with valid consent | Collect unnecessary device or personal data outside permitted consent |
| Issue or ensure delivery of KFS and loan documents | Support digital execution and servicing | Impose charges on the borrower that are payable by the RE to the LSP |
| Ensure direct and traceable fund flow | Facilitate payment interfaces that preserve the permitted flow | Use an LSP-controlled pool or pass-through account for ordinary disbursal or repayment |
| Maintain grievance arrangements and nodal contacts | Operate first-line customer support | Block escalation to the RE or RBI Ombudsman framework |
| Monitor recovery conduct | Perform recovery under the RE mandate | Harass, threaten or use prohibited collection practices |
| Control DLG within the RBI cap and conditions | Provide eligible DLG where permitted | Treat DLG as removal of NPA or provisioning responsibility |
| Apply data-retention and privacy controls | Process data required for the contracted service | Store or reuse borrower data beyond the permitted purpose and retention basis |

The cooling-off period for a digital loan gives the borrower an exit option under the RE's board-approved policy, subject to the minimum regulatory period and payment of principal plus proportionate APR and any permitted one-time processing fee.

## 16. Digital loan money bypasses LSP pool and pass-through accounts

The ordinary rule is direct movement between borrower and RE.

```text
DISBURSAL
RE bank account
→
Borrower's bank account

REPAYMENT
Borrower's bank account
→
RE bank account
```

A third-party LSP account does not sit in the middle merely because the LSP operates the app. The directions contain limited exceptions for specific structures, including permitted end-use disbursals, co-lending flows and other expressly recognised cases. The exception must be the rule's exception, not a commercial workaround.

The same logic governs LSP fees. Charges payable by the RE to an LSP are not collected from the borrower as an undisclosed platform fee. Borrower-borne charges must sit inside the contractual and KFS framework where applicable.

The direct-flow rule creates an audit trail. It aligns the legal lender, the booked asset and the cash movement. It also reduces the risk that an LSP temporarily controls borrower or lender funds.

### Permanent trap

A settlement account is not automatically a permitted pass-through account. The legal basis for every non-direct flow must be identified before the product is launched.

## 17. DLG is capped credit support, not a substitute for prudential recognition

Default Loss Guarantee is limited credit support against a specified digital-lending portfolio. The cap is portfolio-based.

```text
Maximum DLG = 5% x Amount Disbursed from the Specified Portfolio

Maximum DLG          highest permitted guarantee cover
Specified Portfolio  fixed portfolio identified for the DLG arrangement
Amount Disbursed     amount actually disbursed out of that portfolio

Worked: 5% x ₹20 crore = ₹1 crore maximum DLG cover
```

The cap does not mean that each defaulted loan carries a separate 5% guarantee. DLG cannot be offset against individual loans for asset-classification purposes. The RE continues to recognise overdue status, NPA status and provisioning under the applicable prudential rules as though the guarantee did not erase the borrower's default.

The guarantee must be invoked within **120 days of the account becoming overdue**. Invocation changes recovery from the DLG provider. It does not reset the borrower's overdue clock. Recoveries subsequently received from the borrower are dealt with under the contractual DLG arrangement and the RBI directions.

### Never confuse DLG and credit-risk transfer

**DLG** caps first-loss support within the digital-lending framework.

**Loan transfer or securitisation** moves or redistributes an exposure under a separate regulatory framework. A DLG is not a substitute for those rules.

## 18. Co-lending divides funding, economic ownership and income between regulated entities

The Reserve Bank of India (Co-Lending Arrangements) Directions, 2025 create a common framework for eligible regulated entities that jointly fund the same loan.

| Control | Operative treatment |
|---|---|
| Minimum retention | Each participating RE retains at least 10% of each individual loan |
| Borrower pricing | Borrower rate reflects the weighted blend of the participating REs' rates for their funded shares |
| Transfer timing | The agreed share is transferred to the partner RE within 15 calendar days of disbursement |
| Missed transfer window | The loan remains with the originating RE; any later movement follows the Transfer of Loan Exposures Directions |
| Borrower documentation | The co-lending structure, REs, pricing and servicing arrangement are disclosed before commitment |
| Credit information | Each RE reports its share under the applicable CIC requirements |
| Asset quality | Participating REs synchronise borrower-level asset classification for the co-lent exposure |
| Servicing | One RE may service under the agreement, but both retain regulatory responsibility for their share |

The 15-day rule prevents a lender from originating a loan and leaving the eventual economic owner uncertain for an extended period. The co-lending agreement fixes the intended allocation before the loan becomes a seasoning device.

The arrangement is joint lending. It is not a post-origination portfolio sale carried out whenever convenient.

## 19. Co-lending requires account-level allocation, reconciliation and synchronised asset classification

A co-lent loan has one borrower obligation but multiple regulated balance-sheet interests.

```text
Borrower sanctioned ₹10 lakh
→
Originating RE disburses under the agreed co-lending structure
→
Partner RE acquires its agreed share within 15 calendar days
→
Each RE books only its funded economic share
→
Collections flow through the agreed controlled mechanism
→
Principal, interest and charges are allocated by contract
→
Both REs reconcile borrower balance and their individual shares
→
Stress appears
→
Borrower-level asset classification is synchronised across participating REs
```

If RE A holds 70% and RE B holds 30%, a ₹1 lakh principal recovery reduces their principal shares by ₹70,000 and ₹30,000 respectively, subject to the contractual allocation of interest and charges.

Synchronisation is the prudential control. One participant cannot continue to present the same borrower as performing while the other recognises the co-lent exposure as non-performing. Servicing data must therefore move quickly enough to support the same asset-quality state.

### Permanent trap

Treating servicing records as the accounting record. The servicer may collect the cash, but each RE must independently record its share, reconcile balances and meet its own reporting obligations.

## 20. ULI and OCEN are infrastructure rails, not lenders

### Mental model

**ULI moves trusted data and service connections. OCEN standardises digital credit interactions. Banks and NBFCs still lend.**

The Unified Lending Interface is RBI-led digital public infrastructure intended to reduce friction in the movement of consented data and other lending inputs. It connects lenders with permitted data sources through standardised interfaces. It does not sanction a loan and does not become the creditor.

The Open Credit Enablement Network is an open protocol approach for connecting borrower-facing applications, data and lenders. It can standardise how loan products are discovered, requested and processed. It does not create a regulatory licence for an application provider.

The same control stack remains. The borrower provides the required consent. The RE applies its lending policy. KFS and conduct obligations remain. Digital-lending controls remain where the arrangement falls within that framework. Asset classification remains on the lender's books.

The distinction matters because infrastructure can alter distribution without altering legal responsibility. A fast API path can reduce document friction. It cannot convert an unregulated technology provider into a lender.

## 21. Day-end stamping starts the regulatory stress clock

Overdue status is recognised through the lender's day-end process. The account does not wait for month-end review.

```text
Due date arrives
→
Amount remains unpaid at day end
→
Account becomes overdue from that day-end process
→
Days-past-due clock advances with each day-end
→
SMA category changes at the prescribed boundary
→
Product-specific NPA test is reached
→
Borrower-level classification consequences are applied
```

The RBI clarification on day-end processes removed an operational ambiguity that had allowed lenders to describe the same payment delay differently. The due date in the loan agreement and the system's end-of-day status together drive the count.

For a term loan, a payment not made by the specified due date becomes overdue after the day-end process for that date. The count then determines SMA status and, if the overdue continues, NPA recognition. Cure also depends on the amount actually received and the borrower-level rule, not on a manual status override.

This is why asset quality is a systems problem as well as a credit problem. Incorrect due dates, holiday handling or posting cut-offs can misclassify an otherwise correctly understood account.

## 22. SMA-0, SMA-1 and SMA-2 mark early stress before NPA

The operative SMA bands for loans other than revolving facilities are compact.

| Status | Overdue position | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Standard and not SMA | No overdue amount | Contract is current |
| SMA-0 | Up to 30 days | Early overdue |
| SMA-1 | More than 30 days and up to 60 days | Material early stress |
| SMA-2 | More than 60 days and up to 90 days | Immediate pre-NPA stress |
| NPA test | More than 90 days for a term-loan overdue amount | Prudential non-performance |

For cash-credit and overdraft facilities, SMA tagging is based on the period for which the account remains continuously out of order rather than on a scheduled instalment.

SMA is not a substitute for NPA classification. It is the early-stress layer before the product-specific NPA trigger. A borrower can also display non-payment or other credit weakness before a particular account enters an SMA band; the lender's credit controls do not wait for the regulatory label.

### Permanent trap

Using the old 31–60, 61–90 and post-90 sequence. SMA-0 runs **up to 30 days**, SMA-1 **more than 30 up to 60**, and SMA-2 **more than 60 up to 90**.

## 23. NPA recognition uses the test attached to the product

The 90-day rule is not one universal calculation. The product determines what is measured for 90 days, or whether a different test applies.

```text
Identify facility
→
Term loan?
Test principal or interest overdue for more than 90 days
→
CC or OD?
Test whether the account remains out of order for 90 days
→
Bills purchased or discounted?
Test bill overdue for more than 90 days
→
Agricultural advance?
Apply crop-season test
→
Credit card?
Test minimum amount due under the card rule
→
Apply borrower-wise classification across the lender's facilities
```

The error is to store one generic field called `dpd > 90` and assume it classifies every product. A revolving facility can be non-performing because it is continuously out of order even when it has no instalment schedule. A crop loan uses crop seasons, not a simple day count.

The 90-day convention is therefore a regulatory family of tests. The measurement object changes by product.

## 24. Term loans, CC/OD, bills, agricultural advances and cards do not share one NPA test

The deciding condition belongs in one comparison.

| Facility | NPA trigger |
|---|---|
| Term loan | Interest or instalment of principal remains overdue for more than 90 days |
| Cash credit or overdraft | Account remains **out of order** for 90 days |
| CC/OD: limit test | Outstanding remains continuously above sanctioned limit or drawing power for 90 days |
| CC/OD: credit test | No credits continuously for 90 days |
| CC/OD: interest-service test | Credits during the previous 90 days are insufficient to cover interest debited during that period |
| Bills purchased or discounted | Bill remains overdue for more than 90 days |
| Short-duration crop advance | Principal or interest remains overdue for two crop seasons |
| Long-duration crop advance | Principal or interest remains overdue for one crop season |
| Credit card | Minimum Amount Due shown in the statement is not paid fully within 90 days from the due date |

These are recognition tests, not collection strategies. Security value does not extend the threshold. A strong mortgage does not permit a term loan to remain standard after the overdue test is met.

The agricultural rule applies to loans granted for agricultural purposes covered by the crop-season framework. Other agricultural borrowers or activities can fall under the ordinary 90-day rule depending on the facility.

## 25. Borrower-wise classification spreads NPA status across facilities

Commercial-bank IRACP classification is generally borrower-wise, not facility-wise. When one credit facility to a borrower becomes NPA, the bank classifies the other credit facilities granted to that borrower as NPA as well, subject to specific regulatory exceptions.

The rule prevents cosmetic splitting. A borrower cannot appear performing merely because one overdraft remains within limit while a term loan with the same bank is more than 90 days overdue. The lender's exposure to the borrower is treated as a connected credit state.

Multiple banking and consortium arrangements are different. Each lender applies the classification based on the recovery record in its own books and the applicable inter-creditor or co-lending rules. A borrower's NPA status at Bank A does not automatically stamp every facility at Bank B as NPA. Co-lending is tighter because the participating REs must synchronise borrower-level classification for the co-lent arrangement.

Certain fully secured advances against specified own deposits or financial instruments receive special treatment under IRACP. Central-government-guaranteed advances also have a specific NPA recognition treatment linked to invocation and repudiation of the guarantee.

### Never confuse borrower-wise classification and system-wide classification

Borrower-wise classification operates within the lender. It is not an automatic industry-wide NPA flag across unrelated lenders.

## 26. Sub-standard, doubtful and loss follow NPA ageing and recoverability

NPA recognition begins the classification chain. It does not end it.

```text
Standard asset
→
Product-specific NPA trigger met
→
Sub-standard asset
remains sub-standard for up to 12 months
→
Doubtful asset
has remained in the sub-standard category for 12 months
→
Doubtful ageing increases
secured-portion provision rises with time
→
Loss asset
identified as uncollectible or of such little value that continuance as a bankable asset is not warranted
```

A **sub-standard asset** is an NPA that has remained in that category for a period not exceeding 12 months. A **doubtful asset** is one that has remained sub-standard for 12 months. A **loss asset** is identified by the bank, auditors or RBI as uncollectible, although the amount may not yet have been written off in full.

The categories do two jobs. They communicate deterioration and they determine minimum provisioning. The move from sub-standard to doubtful is driven by time in NPA status. The move to loss can be driven by identified uncollectibility rather than waiting for a further ageing band.

## 27. Asset classification and provisioning are separate decisions

### Permanent rule

Classification answers **what state the asset is in**. Provisioning answers **how much loss allowance the prudential rule requires against that state**.

Collateral does not keep a defaulted loan standard. A ₹1 crore term loan secured by property worth ₹2 crore still becomes NPA when the overdue test is met. Security matters later, principally when the provision on a doubtful asset is split between secured and unsecured portions.

The reverse error also occurs. A loan can remain standard and still require a standard-asset provision. Provision is not evidence that the loan is NPA. It is a prudential buffer required even before non-performance.

### Permanent trap

Netting expected collateral recovery against the overdue clock. Asset classification is determined first. Security and provisioning are applied after classification under the relevant rule.

## 28. Provisioning rises with asset class, security and seasoning

For commercial banks under the IRACP regime in force on 1 September 2026, the main minimum provisioning schedule is:

| Asset state | Provisioning treatment |
|---|---:|
| Standard: direct agriculture and SME advances | 0.25% |
| Standard: commercial real estate, CRE | 1.00% |
| Standard: commercial real estate - residential housing, CRE-RH | 0.75% |
| Standard: other standard advances | 0.40% |
| Sub-standard: secured exposure | 15% |
| Sub-standard: unsecured exposure | 25% total |
| Sub-standard: qualifying unsecured infrastructure exposure with prescribed safeguards | 20% total |
| Doubtful: unsecured portion | 100% |
| Doubtful: secured portion, up to 1 year in doubtful category | 25% |
| Doubtful: secured portion, more than 1 year and up to 3 years | 40% |
| Doubtful: secured portion, more than 3 years | 100% |
| Loss asset | 100% |

The standard-asset rate follows the exposure category. It is not a general secured-versus-unsecured split. The NPA rates then depend on classification, security and doubtful ageing.

The rates are regulatory minima. A bank can carry a higher provision under its policy or another applicable prudential requirement.

## 29. Regulatory provision is the prescribed rate applied to the prescribed base

A doubtful asset illustrates why the calculation must separate secured and unsecured portions.

```text
Required Provision = Unsecured Portion x 100% + Secured Portion x Doubtful Rate

Unsecured Portion  outstanding not covered by realisable security value
Secured Portion    outstanding covered by realisable security value recognised for provisioning
Doubtful Rate      25%, 40% or 100% according to time in doubtful category

Worked:
Outstanding = ₹80 lakh
Realisable security = ₹50 lakh
Unsecured portion = ₹30 lakh
Time in doubtful category = 2 years

Provision = ₹30 lakh x 100% + ₹50 lakh x 40%
          = ₹30 lakh + ₹20 lakh
          = ₹50 lakh
```

The security value used for provisioning is the realisable value recognised under the prudential rules, not the original purchase price or a convenient collateral estimate.

A sub-standard asset uses a different schedule. A secured sub-standard exposure attracts 15% on the total outstanding. An unsecured sub-standard exposure attracts 25% in total, subject to the specific infrastructure concession where its conditions are met.

Provisioning is calculated after classification. It cannot be used to reverse the classification itself.

## 30. Income recognition changes when an asset becomes non-performing

Interest on an NPA is not recognised on accrual merely because the contract continues to charge it. The prudential rule shifts recognition to realisation.

When an advance becomes NPA, unrealised interest that was previously credited to income is reversed or provided for according to the IRACP requirements. Subsequent interest income on the NPA is recognised when actually realised, subject to the prescribed treatment of recoveries.

This creates a deliberate break between the legal receivable and reported income. The loan agreement can continue to accrue contractual interest against the borrower while the bank stops recognising that interest as earned income for prudential financial reporting.

Recoveries on NPAs are appropriated according to the bank's policy and the regulatory framework. The accounting treatment must not create income from amounts that have not been collected.

### Never confuse contractual accrual and income recognition

**Contractual accrual** determines what the borrower owes under the loan contract.

**Prudential income recognition** determines what the bank may recognise as income while the asset is non-performing.

## 31. Upgrade requires full borrower-level cure

### Permanent rule

An NPA returns to standard only after the borrower pays the **entire arrears of interest and principal** relating to all credit facilities with that lender that are caught by the borrower-wise rule.

A token payment does not upgrade the account. Bringing only the oldest instalment inside 90 days does not upgrade it. Clearing one facility while another facility with the same lender remains in arrears does not complete the borrower-level cure.

The rule matters operationally because payment allocation can change apparent DPD without completing the prudential requirement. The servicing system must test the full arrears position before the classification flag is removed.

Restructured assets have an additional path. Their upgrade depends on the performance conditions of the applicable restructuring or resolution framework. A restructuring event is not itself a cure.

### Permanent trap

Using the pre-NPA delinquency logic for an NPA upgrade. Before NPA, a payment can move an account back through DPD bands. After NPA, the upgrade test is full regulatory cure, not merely a lower DPD number.

## 32. Every asset-quality threshold belongs in one deterioration and provisioning chain

The current commercial-bank IRACP chain can be held in one table.

| Rule | Threshold or period | Result or minimum provision |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overdue starts | Contractual due date, after day-end | Overdue clock begins |
| SMA-0 | Up to 30 days overdue | Early stress |
| SMA-1 | More than 30 up to 60 days | Early stress |
| SMA-2 | More than 60 up to 90 days | Pre-NPA stress |
| Term loan | More than 90 days overdue | NPA |
| CC/OD | Out of order for 90 days | NPA |
| Bills | More than 90 days overdue | NPA |
| Credit card | Minimum Amount Due unpaid fully within 90 days from due date | NPA |
| Short-duration crop | 2 crop seasons overdue | NPA |
| Long-duration crop | 1 crop season overdue | NPA |
| Sub-standard | Up to 12 months after NPA | 15% secured; 25% unsecured; qualifying safeguarded unsecured infrastructure 20% |
| Doubtful entry | After 12 months sub-standard | Unsecured portion 100% |
| Doubtful secured portion | Up to 1 year doubtful | 25% |
| Doubtful secured portion | More than 1 up to 3 years doubtful | 40% |
| Doubtful secured portion | More than 3 years doubtful | 100% |
| Loss | On identification as loss | 100% |
| Standard agriculture and SME | While standard | 0.25% |
| Standard CRE | While standard | 1.00% |
| Standard CRE-RH | While standard | 0.75% |
| Other standard advances | While standard | 0.40% |

The same number can govern different objects. Ninety days overdue on a term loan is not the same test as 90 days out of order on a CC/OD account.

## 33. Restructuring and resolution do not reset asset quality by themselves

The Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets separates recognition of default from the choice of resolution plan.

```text
Default occurs
→
Applicable lenders begin review
→
30-day review period for covered exposures
→
Resolution strategy selected
bilateral cure, restructuring, change in ownership, sale, enforcement or insolvency
→
If restructuring or change in ownership is used
apply implementation conditions and asset-classification consequences
→
Monitor satisfactory performance
→
Upgrade only when the applicable cure or restructuring conditions are met
```

Where multiple lenders are involved, the inter-creditor framework governs collective implementation for covered cases. Failure to implement within the regulatory timeline attracts additional provisioning. The framework is designed to prevent delay being economically free.

Restructuring means the lender grants a concession because of the borrower's financial difficulty, such as changing payment timing, tenor or other economic terms. It is not an automatic return to standard classification. The account carries the classification and provisioning consequences specified by the applicable resolution rule until the prescribed performance conditions are satisfied.

### Permanent trap

Calling a repayment extension a cure. If the concession meets the regulatory definition of restructuring, the restructuring rules apply even when the revised instalments are paid on time immediately after modification.

## 34. Write-off removes accounting carrying value, not the legal debt claim

A write-off is an accounting and prudential action. A waiver is a legal or contractual surrender of the claim. They are not the same event.

A **technical write-off** removes the recognised loan balance from the books at the head-office level or through the bank's accounting treatment without giving up recovery rights. The borrower can still be pursued. Security can still be enforced. Recoveries after write-off are recognised according to the applicable accounting policy.

A **compromise settlement** is different. The lender agrees to accept a negotiated amount in full and final settlement, subject to the settlement terms and approvals. Once the borrower performs the settlement, the residual claim is extinguished to the extent agreed.

RBI's compromise-settlement framework requires a board-approved policy and governance controls. Settlements with wilful defaulters or fraud accounts do not erase criminal proceedings or other legal consequences. Where payment under a compromise settlement is spread beyond the permitted short settlement window, restructuring treatment can be triggered.

### Never confuse write-off and waiver

**Write-off:** balance-sheet recognition changes; recovery rights can remain.

**Waiver or completed compromise:** the lender gives up the agreed residual claim.

## 35. Recovery route depends on security, debtor form and desired control

Recovery is a route-selection problem. The lender first determines what legal right exists and what outcome is required.

A secured lender seeking possession or sale of collateral can use SARFAESI where the Act applies. A bank or notified financial institution seeking a tribunal recovery certificate can proceed through the Debt Recovery Tribunal where the statutory monetary jurisdiction is met. A financial creditor facing corporate default can use the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code to place the debtor into a collective insolvency process once the statutory threshold and admission requirements are met. A compromise settlement remains consensual and can be used where a negotiated recovery produces a better governed outcome.

The routes can interact. Admission of a corporate insolvency resolution process creates a moratorium that stays individual enforcement actions, including SARFAESI measures against the corporate debtor. A lender can also pursue guarantee rights subject to the governing law and the effect of the insolvency process.

The decision therefore depends on five questions: **is there enforceable security, who is the debtor, what amount is in default, is collective control required, and is a negotiated exit viable**.

## 36. SARFAESI, DRT, IBC and compromise settlement solve different recovery problems

| Route | Trigger | Security required? | Timeline | Creditor control | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SARFAESI | Secured debt in default with statutory conditions satisfied; account ordinarily classified NPA before section 13 enforcement | Yes | 60-day demand notice before section 13(4) measures; completion depends on enforcement and sale | High for secured creditor, subject to statutory procedure and DRT challenge | Possession, management measures and sale of secured asset |
| DRT | Debt due to a bank or eligible financial institution above the statutory jurisdiction threshold | No | Tribunal process; no single guaranteed completion period | Creditor prosecutes recovery claim before tribunal | Recovery certificate and execution against debtor assets |
| IBC corporate insolvency | Corporate default of at least ₹1 crore and admission by NCLT | No | 180-day CIRP, extendable; statutory outer limit 330 days including permitted extensions and litigation | Collective through Committee of Creditors; resolution plan approval requires 66% voting share | Resolution plan, change of control or liquidation |
| Compromise settlement | Negotiated resolution of a stressed or defaulted exposure under board-approved policy | No | Agreed between lender and borrower; payment structure governed by policy and RBI framework | Consensual; lender retains approval control until settlement | Agreed recovery and discharge of residual claim on completion |

DRT's current pecuniary jurisdiction for bank and financial-institution recovery applications begins at **₹20 lakh**. SARFAESI is not a route for ordinary unsecured enforcement. IBC is not a bilateral collection tool; admission shifts control into a collective insolvency framework and imposes a moratorium.

### Key insight

The fastest legal power is not always the best recovery route. Security, enterprise value, inter-creditor conflict and the need for a going-concern solution determine the choice.

## 37. Recovery agents remain the lender's responsibility throughout collection

### Permanent rule

A bank or NBFC is responsible for the conduct of the recovery agents it appoints.

Recovery cannot use intimidation, humiliation, threats, persistent harassment or misleading representations. Contact with borrowers must stay within permitted hours. RBI's 2022 recovery-agent restrictions prohibit calls before **8:00 a.m.** and after **7:00 p.m.** for recovery of overdue loans.

Agents must identify themselves and act under the lender's authorised process. The lender must maintain oversight, grievance handling and escalation. Digital origination does not dilute these controls. An LSP or outsourced call centre performing recovery is still inside the RE's conduct perimeter.

Borrower protection does not suspend the debt. It constrains the means of collection. A lender may send lawful notices, invoke contractual remedies, enforce security, file proceedings or negotiate settlement. It may not substitute harassment for legal process.

### Permanent trap

Treating a third-party collection vendor as an arm's-length contractor for conduct risk. The borrower sees the agent as acting for the lender, and RBI supervises the lender on that basis.

## 38. For commercial banks, 2025 IRACP remains operative through 31 March 2027; ECL begins 1 April 2027

As at **1 September 2026**, commercial banks continue to apply the **Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025** for the IRACP regime described in this document.

The **Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Asset Classification, Provisioning and Income Recognition) Directions, 2026** take effect on **1 April 2027**. They replace the existing incurred-loss-style provisioning architecture for covered commercial banks with an expected credit loss framework while retaining a regulatory asset-classification architecture.

That future rule is a transition point, not the current regime. The SMA, NPA, borrower-wise classification, income-recognition and provisioning discussion above therefore states the position operative on 1 September 2026. It does not apply the 2027 ECL measurement framework early.

The future framework changes how loss allowance is measured. The mechanics of ECL measurement, staging, forward-looking scenarios and model governance belong to the expected-credit-loss subject and are not taught here.

### Key insight

A future effective direction can be known and still be non-operative. Regulatory analysis uses the rule effective on the reporting or decision date.

## What to retain

- Lender type is the first regulatory classification; product and channel come next.
- Payments banks do not lend from their own balance sheet; banks, lending NBFCs and HFCs operate under different prudential perimeters.
- PSL is measured through lender-level targets and sub-targets; PSLCs transfer regulatory achievement, not loans.
- Digital lending leaves accountability with the RE. LSPs distribute and service within that perimeter.
- DLG is capped at 5% of the amount disbursed from the specified portfolio and does not remove NPA or provisioning responsibility.
- Co-lending requires at least 10% retention by each participating RE, transfer within 15 calendar days and synchronised borrower-level asset classification.
- SMA-0 is up to 30 days, SMA-1 is more than 30 up to 60, and SMA-2 is more than 60 up to 90.
- NPA recognition is product-specific: term loans, CC/OD, bills, crop loans and credit cards do not use one identical test.
- Commercial-bank NPA classification is generally borrower-wise. Upgrade requires full cure across affected facilities with the lender.
- Provisioning is separate from classification. Security affects provision; it does not postpone NPA recognition.
- SARFAESI, DRT, IBC and compromise settlement are different recovery routes, not interchangeable labels for default management.
- The 2025 commercial-bank IRACP regime remains operative through 31 March 2027. The 2026 ECL framework begins on 1 April 2027.
